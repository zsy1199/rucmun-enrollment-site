Introduction:
	According to the latest data released by the United Nationsin 2025, by 2050 nearly 45% of the global urban population isexpected to be exposed to extreme heat conditions. Heatwaveshave already become the deadliest climate-related hazard,causing more fatalities annually than hurricanes, tornadoes, andlightning combined. Extreme heat is no longer merely ameteorological phenomenon; it has evolved into a systemicurban risk affecting public health, housing security, energy
systems, water supply, labor productivity, and social stability.
In 2025, several national-level heat action plans, urbanresilience strategies, and UN system reports, including theUnited Nations Human Settlements Programme(UNHSP), werereleased or updated. These documents collectively emphasizethe need to integrate heat risk management into urban planning,housing design, land use regulation, and social protection mechanisms. Within this context, UNHSP plays a critical role intranslating climate risk knowledge into people-centered urbanpolicies, promoting heat-resilient human settlements, andcoordinating with other UN agencies to support member states,particularly developing countries, in building adaptive urbansystems.
